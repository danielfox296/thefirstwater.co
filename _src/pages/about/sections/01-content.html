I'm Daniel, also known as Firstwater. I compose the sound for each night, play live instruments over it, and mix it from the floor, lying where you’ll lie. Firstwater is where I do that in person, with people beside me.

The practice

How a night is built

Each night is a group of people on the floor in low light, with bass you feel in your chest, a formal opening and a formal close, and seventy-five minutes with nothing to do and nothing to say. I compose the sound for it beforehand and play live instruments over it in the space.

Each night is built around something worth putting down. One is for the end of a relationship, one is for grief, one is for the week after a layoff, and the sessions page lists the rest.

The craft

Slow, thoughtful attentiveness to sound

A space can sound like a gymnasium or it can sound like the inside of a cello, and the difference between the two is work I do before anyone arrives: I tune the sound system to the space, set the bass so you feel it in your chest, and build the seventy-five minutes with a beginning, a middle and an end.

The sound is only one part of the night. The words at the open and the close matter as much, and so does the ritual, and so do the people on the floor beside you. I bring the same attentiveness to all of it, because the night is something we make together.

I learned what sound does in a space from the stage, years before I could measure it, and I’ve spent the years since studying how it moves people. Firstwater is the closest I’ve come to using all of that at once.

See the sessions

The sessions that we hold at Firstwater in Denver gather people from all walks of life and states into a place of gratitude, stillness, and joy. Each ceremony uses expert facilitation, healing sound, and sometimes movement and exercises to provide an opening that didn't exist before you got there. Come exactly as you please. This is a place where you can lay down things that don't serve you, or leave a completely different person.
